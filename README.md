## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Expected Solution: Create a digital marketplace that:

• Connects farmers/FPOs directly with consumers and bulk buyers.
• Provides logistics support.
• Uses AI for demand forecasting and route optimization.

Benefits:

• Better prices for farmers.
• Lower prices for consumers.
• Reduced supply chain inefficiencies.

## Proposed Solution
1. Proposed Solution

Our proposed solution is DirectFarm, a digital agricultural marketplace designed to connect farmers, Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs), consumers, retailers, and bulk buyers directly.

The platform aims to eliminate unnecessary intermediaries by allowing farmers to list their agricultural products, set prices, and sell directly to buyers. Consumers can browse available products, compare prices, and purchase fresh agricultural produce at affordable rates.

The platform will include:

Direct farmer-to-consumer and farmer-to-business connections.

AI-based demand forecasting to predict market requirements.

Smart logistics management for efficient transportation.

Transparent pricing and digital payment facilities.

Real-time tracking of orders and deliveries.

Regional language support for farmers.

2. How It Addresses the Problem

Currently, agricultural products pass through multiple intermediaries before reaching consumers. Each intermediary adds additional costs, reducing farmers' earnings and increasing consumer prices.

DirectFarm addresses this issue by:

Reducing unnecessary middlemen through direct transactions.

Helping farmers receive better prices for their produce.

Providing consumers with fresh products at competitive prices.

Connecting farmers with restaurants, supermarkets, retailers, and wholesalers.

Using AI to identify demand and reduce unnecessary transportation and wastage.

3. Innovation and Uniqueness of the Solution

The uniqueness of DirectFarm lies in combining digital marketplace facilities with artificial intelligence and logistics management.

Innovative features include:

AI Demand Prediction: Predicts future demand using historical sales and market trends.

Smart Price Recommendations: Helps farmers make informed pricing decisions using available market information.

Route Optimization: Identifies efficient delivery routes to reduce transportation time and costs.

Group Buying: Allows consumers and small retailers to combine orders and purchase directly from farmers.

Multilingual Interface: Supports regional languages to make the platform accessible to rural communities.

Farmer Verification: Helps buyers identify verified farmers and FPOs.

## Technical Approach
The proposed system will use the following technologies:

Frontend:

HTML, CSS and JavaScript.

React.js for the web application.

Flutter for the mobile application.

Backend:

Python with Flask or FastAPI.

Node.js for additional backend services.

REST APIs for communication between modules.

Database:

MySQL or PostgreSQL for user, product, order, and transaction details.

Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning:

Python and Scikit-learn for demand forecasting.

Machine learning models for analysing historical sales and seasonal trends.

Optimization algorithms for delivery route planning.

Data analytics for price and demand insights.

Additional Technologies:

GPS integration for location tracking.

Digital payment gateway integration.

Cloud hosting for scalability.

Authentication and encryption for data security.

Working Process:

Farmers register and upload their available agricultural products.

Buyers browse products and place orders.

The AI system analyses demand and provides recommendations.

The logistics module connects orders with suitable transportation options.

Buyers track their orders until delivery.

Farmers receive payments directly through the platform.

DirectFarm is technically and economically feasible because it can be developed using affordable, widely available technologies and existing digital infrastructure.


## Feasibility and Viability
Feasibility:

The platform can initially be developed as a web and mobile application.

Existing cloud services can support data storage and application hosting.

Open-source AI and machine learning tools can be used to develop forecasting models.

GPS and mapping services can support delivery planning.

Potential Challenges:

Limited digital literacy among some farmers.

Internet connectivity issues in rural areas.

Difficulty coordinating transportation and deliveries.

Maintaining trust between farmers and buyers.

Ensuring accurate product quality and availability information.

Solutions to Challenges:

Provide a simple interface with regional language support.

Introduce assisted registration through FPOs and local agricultural organizations.

Partner with existing logistics providers.

Implement farmer and buyer verification.

Provide customer support and transparent order tracking.

Viability:

The platform can generate revenue through small transaction fees, optional logistics services, and subscription plans for bulk buyers. Basic registration and product listing can remain free for farmers.

Social Impact:
